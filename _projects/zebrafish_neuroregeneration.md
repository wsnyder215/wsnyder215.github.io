## Summary
As one of the few vertebrates capable of robust neurogenesis throughout their lifespan, zebrafish are an ideal model for studying the mechanisms of neural recovery and regeneration. This is particularly true of the zebrafish olfactory system. Comprised of the olfactory bulb (OB) and epithelium (OE), their olfactory system exhibits extensive neuroplasticity in response to cell damage. In fact, previous research has found that these regions exhibit complete physiological and functional recovery by 21 days after lesion. 

However, the exact mechanisms by which this recovery takes place are not well understood. In this project, we utilized a novel model of excitotoxic injury in the zebrafish OB in order to study the mechanisms of recovery over time. We induced damage in adult zebrafish via a unilateral focal excitotoxic quinolinic-acid-derived lesion, then subsequently tracked markers of neurogenesis and cell inflammation using immunohistochemical assays. 

We observed extensive neurodegeneration throughout the olfactory system, as well as increased neuroinflammation and new cell markers. We also found progressive recovery of the tissue with transient neurodegeneration markers throughout recovery. These results contribute to the understanding of the neurological mechanisms and associations between inflammation, neurogenesis, and neuronal recovery following damage.
